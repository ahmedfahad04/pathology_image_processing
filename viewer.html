📚 How the paper found the mutant (in plain words)
  • Doctors took the same tissue that made the glass slide and checked the DNA for spelling mistakes in the FGFR3 gene.
  • If they found a known harmful spelling mistake, they marked the patient as "mutant". If not, they marked them "normal".
  • Then they showed the computer the whole slide image with just one label — like showing a photo of a class and saying "this class has a student with a red hat" without pointing to who.
  • The computer learned by itself which small pieces of the image look most like "mutant" — that's how it later makes a colour heatmap.
🔍 What we have here — and what we don't
  • We HAVE: for every image, whether it is mutant or normal, which spelling mistake it is, and basic patient info (like stage, age) — all saved in data/.
  • We DON'T have: a box or coloured spot inside the image that says "mutant is here". No file tells the exact pixels.
  • What you can do here at most: browse the list, filter mutant / normal, see the details, and click Locate in Viewer → to open the full slide. Remember: if a slide is called mutant, the whole slide counts as mutant — the mistake is in the DNA, not in one visible dot.
